Most people try AI once or twice, get something bland back, and decide it isn't for them.

I don't think the tool is the problem. I think nobody showed them how to use it.

So this newsletter is for that. Each issue is something I've built with AI, or something I've learned using it, written in plain English for people who aren't developers. I'm not one either. I run marketing and customer service teams, and I've spent this year finding out what AI can actually do on a normal working Tuesday.

## The four habits

If you're new to this, the best place to start is a simple framework Anthropic teaches in its free AI Fluency course. It boils being good with AI down to four habits, all beginning with D:

1. **Delegation:** deciding what to hand over to AI, and what to keep.
2. **Description:** saying clearly what you want, like you'd brief a smart colleague.
3. **Discernment:** judging what comes back. Is it right? Is it actually good?
4. **Diligence:** using it responsibly, and owning the result.

[Image: four-ds.png]

Most people only ever practise the second one, and only a little. That's why the results disappoint.

## A quick example

Say you need to reply to a difficult email.

- **Delegation:** let AI draft it. Keep the decision about what to say for yourself.
- **Description:** tell it the background, who it's going to, the tone you want, and how long it should be.
- **Discernment:** read the draft as the person receiving it. Check every fact.
- **Diligence:** don't paste in anyone's private details, and send it as yourself, because it's yours.

Ten minutes, instead of an evening of dreading it.
