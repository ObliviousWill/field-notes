Most AI at work is one person and one chat window. You ask, it answers, and the benefit stays with you.

I wanted something the whole team could benefit from: AI helpers that do useful groundwork every morning, whether or not anyone remembers to ask. This post is about what I learned setting them up. The [project page](/field-notes/projects/ai-agent-team/) has the details.

## A quick explanation first

An "agent" is an AI that does a task on a schedule, instead of waiting for someone to type a question. Ours run early on weekday mornings. Each one looks at one area of the business and decides whether there's anything worth raising.

There are six, plus one that checks their work:

- **The bookings helper** looks at how bookings are going.
- **The enquiries helper** helps make sure every new enquiry gets a quick, helpful reply.
- **The search helper** looks at how people find us on Google.
- **The website helper** checks our pages are accurate.
- **The writer** suggests articles and prepares first drafts for us to edit.
- **The checker** reviews everything before anyone sees it.

## Lesson 1: design around people's attention

It's easy to get AI to produce a lot. Reports, ideas, analysis, every day. But nobody has time to read all of it, and a pile of unread suggestions helps no one.

So the most important rule is: **at most one suggestion per helper per day.** It has to be the one that would help most. If nothing is worth raising, raising nothing counts as a good day.

## Lesson 2: give each helper one clear focus

A long list of tasks doesn't work well. What works is giving each helper **one area to look after**, and a clear description of what "useful" looks like there.

Then make sure it has what it needs to do that well:

1. a clear focus
2. access to the right information, and nothing more
3. set times to run
4. an owner
5. a check on its work
6. a memory of past feedback

## Lesson 3: separate the doer from the checker

We don't proofread our own writing, because it's hard to spot your own mistakes. The same applies to AI.

So there's a separate checker. It can't change anything. It can only pass work, or send it back with reasons. It re-checks numbers against the original source rather than trusting the summary.

## Lesson 4: people make the decisions

None of the helpers can approve, publish or change anything. They prepare. People decide. That matters most when a decision affects customers, money or anyone's work.

We're in a careful trial at the moment: the helpers can read and suggest, nothing more. Anything beyond that would be a deliberate, explicit decision.

## Lesson 5: fit into how the team already works

Nobody has to learn a new tool. Suggestions appear on the task board the team already uses, and can be accepted or turned down in one click, with a short reason. The helpers read those reasons, so they get more useful over time.

## What I'd tell anyone trying this

- **Start with the rules, not the technology.** What can it do, what can't it do, and who decides?
- **Fewer, better suggestions beat more output.**
- **Separate the work from the checking.**
- **Make it easy to say no.** A suggestion that's quick to turn down is a suggestion people will actually read.

The point isn't to do more. It's to give the team better information, earlier, so they can spend their time on the work that matters most.
