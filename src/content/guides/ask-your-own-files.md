Claude can read faster than any of us. Give it a 40-page document or a spreadsheet with thousands of rows, and it can tell you what's in it in seconds.

But reading fast isn't the same as reading right. This guide is about getting the speed without losing the accuracy.

## Getting your files in

There are two ways:

- **Attach it.** Drag the file into the chat, or use the attach button. This works for documents, PDFs, spreadsheets and images.
- **Connect your storage.** If you've connected your file storage (guide 8), you can ask Claude to find and read a document without attaching it.

## Good questions for a document

- "What are the five most important things in this?"
- "What does this say about holiday entitlement?"
- "Is anything in here unclear, or does it contradict itself?"
- "What questions would a new reader have after reading this?"

The third one is underrated. Claude is good at spotting gaps and contradictions that the author was too close to see.

## Good questions for a spreadsheet

- "What patterns do you see in this?"
- "Which month was busiest, and by how much?"
- "What looks unusual, and what should I double-check?"

## The checking part

Every time, follow up with:

1. **"Where did you find that?"** Then go to that place in the file and read it yourself.
2. **"Show me how you worked that out."** For any number, check at least one calculation by hand.
3. **"What did you leave out?"** Summaries are, by definition, missing things. Make sure nothing important is among them.

Large files can also be too big for it to read in full. If something seems to be missing, ask: "Did you read the whole file?"

## Be careful what you share

Don't upload files with other people's personal details, such as staff records, customer lists or anything about a specific person, unless you're using a tool your organisation has approved for exactly that. (More in guide 11.)

## Try this today

Take a long document you've been meaning to read. Ask Claude three questions about it. For one of the answers, find the exact place in the file that proves it.
