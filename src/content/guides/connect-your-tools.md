So far, Claude has only known what you've told it or pasted in. That's a real limit. It can't see your inbox, your calendar or your documents, so you have to copy things across.

**Connectors** change that. A connector lets Claude look at a tool you already use, so you can ask about your own work instead of pasting it in.

## What that looks like

Once you've connected, say, your email and calendar, you can ask things like:

- "Which emails do I need to reply to today?"
- "What's on my calendar this week, and what do I need to prepare?"
- "Find the latest version of the project plan."
- "Summarise everything about the conference since Monday."

You can also combine sources: "Look at my calendar for tomorrow and my recent emails with those people, and give me a short brief for each meeting."

## How to connect

In Claude, look for **Connectors** in settings. You'll see a list of tools you can connect, such as email, calendars, file storage and many popular work apps. Connecting usually means signing in to that tool and agreeing what Claude can see.

If you use Claude through work, your organisation may decide which connectors are available. If you're not sure, ask before connecting anything.

## Reading and doing

Some connectors only let Claude **read** things. Others let it **do** things too, such as creating a draft email or adding a calendar event.

A good habit, whatever the connector: **ask for a draft, read what it plans to do, and only then say yes.** A draft email in your inbox is fine. An email sent without you reading it isn't.

## Check where it came from

When Claude answers from your own information, the checking habit from guide 5 matters even more, because the answer *looks* authoritative:

- Ask **"Where did that come from?"** and open the source.
- Check one figure against the original.
- Ask whether it might have missed anything, or used something out of date.
- Be precise with your words. Terms like "this month's sales" or "active customers" can mean different things in different places. Say exactly what you mean.

## Connect only what you need

This is Diligence (guide 11) in action:

- Start with one connection, not ten.
- Connect what the job needs, and nothing more.
- If you're sharing your screen, remember Claude may show information from your connected tools. Close anything private first.

## Try this today

Connect one tool, such as your calendar. Ask one question about your own week. Then check one thing in the answer against the original.
