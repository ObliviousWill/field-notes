Before we get into how to ask Claude for things, there's a bigger question. What should you ask it to do at all?

## The four Ds

Anthropic, the company that makes Claude, teaches a free course on AI fluency, built with two university professors, Rick Dakan and Joseph Feller. At its heart are four habits, all starting with D:

1. **Delegation:** deciding what to hand over to AI, and what to keep.
2. **Description:** saying clearly what you want.
3. **Discernment:** judging what comes back.
4. **Diligence:** using it responsibly, and owning the result.

The rest of these guides follow those four. This one is about the first.

## Why delegation comes first

Most people get this backwards. They open Claude, ask the first thing that comes into their head, and then decide whether AI is any good based on the answer.

A better way is to start with the job, not the tool. Ask yourself three questions.

**1. What am I actually trying to do?** Not "write an email", but "get my landlord to fix the boiler this week without falling out with him". When you're clear on the goal, you can tell whether help is helping.

**2. What is AI good at, and bad at?** Claude is very good at drafting, summarising, explaining, rewording, brainstorming and spotting gaps. It's weaker at up-to-the-minute facts, exact sums done in its head, and anything where it can't see what you can see. It also doesn't know your situation unless you tell it.

**3. Which parts need me?** Some parts of a job need your judgement, your relationships or your accountability. Those stay with you.

## Three ways to work together

The course describes three ways of working with AI. In plain terms:

- **It does the job for you.** "Turn these notes into a tidy list." You check it and move on.
- **You do the job together.** "Help me think through whether to take this job offer." It's a back-and-forth, and you lead.
- **It does the job on its own, on your behalf.** "Every Monday, find this week's news in my industry and email me a summary." You set it up, then it runs. (I wrote about [doing exactly this](/field-notes/blog/your-first-ai-job-should-be-boring/).)

Most of your use will be the second one. That's fine. It's often where the most value is.

## A simple way to split a job

Take any task and split it into three piles:

| Hand it over | Do it together | Keep it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First drafts | Decisions | The final say |
| Summaries | Planning | Anything sensitive |
| Rewording | Tricky messages | Relationships |

For example, writing a reference for a colleague. Claude can draft it from your notes (hand over). You decide together what to emphasise (together). You check every claim is true and sign it (keep).

## Try this today

Pick one task you've got on this week. Split it into the three piles above. Hand over the first pile, and notice how much quicker the rest goes.
