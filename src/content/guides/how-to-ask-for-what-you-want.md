Most disappointing answers from AI have the same cause: it didn't know something you knew. It can't read your mind. It can only read your message.

This is the second of the four Ds: **Description**. The fix is simple. Stop asking questions like you would in Google, and start giving briefs like you would to a smart new colleague.

## The difference

A question:

> Write a thank-you message for my team.

You'll get something polite, generic and forgettable.

A brief:

> Write a short thank-you message to my team of six. We've just finished a really hard month: two people were off sick and everyone covered. I want it to sound like me: warm, a bit informal, not cheesy. Mention that I noticed the extra hours. It's going in our group chat, so keep it under 60 words.

Same request. A completely different answer, because now it knows what you know.

## The five things to include

You don't need all five every time, but the more you include, the better the answer.

1. **What you want.** "A thank-you message."
2. **Who it's for.** "My team of six, in our group chat."
3. **The background.** "A hard month, two people off, everyone covered."
4. **How it should feel or sound.** "Warm, informal, not cheesy."
5. **Any limits.** "Under 60 words."

A handy test: if you handed your message to a person, could they do the job without asking you a single question? If not, add what they'd ask.

## Three things to describe

Anthropic's course splits Description into three parts. They're worth knowing because most people only do the first:

- **The result:** what you want back. "A thank-you message, under 60 words."
- **The method:** how you want it to go about it. "Ask me questions first." "Think it through step by step." "Give me three options, then recommend one."
- **The manner:** how you want it to behave with you. "Be blunt." "Challenge me if I'm wrong." "Explain it like I'm new to this."

## Give it the raw material

If you have notes, an old email, a document or a messy list, paste the lot in, or attach the file. Don't summarise it first. Your summary throws away detail Claude could have used.

You can also attach photos. A picture of a letter, a screenshot of an error, or a photo of the back of a boiler all work.

## Talk instead of type

If typing a long brief feels like a chore, talk. Most phones have a microphone button on the keyboard, and the Claude app lets you speak. When you talk, you naturally give more background, and the answers get better. I wrote [a whole post about this](/field-notes/blog/get-twice-as-fast-at-typing-without-typing/).

## Then improve it together

The first answer is a first draft. Tell it what to change:

- "Good, but shorter."
- "The second paragraph is too formal."
- "Give me three different opening lines to choose from."
- "What have I missed?"

The people who get the most from AI are the ones who argue with it.

## Ask for its opinion

Claude is good at pushing back if you invite it. Try:

- "What's the weakest part of this?"
- "What would a sceptical reader say?"
- "Give me your single best recommendation, not a list of options."

## Try this today

Take something you asked Claude recently that came back a bit flat. Ask it again, this time with all five things from the list above. Compare the two answers.
