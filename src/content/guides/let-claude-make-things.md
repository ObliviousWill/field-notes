Most people only use Claude for answers. But it can also make things you can use straight away. This is the point where it stops being a clever chat and starts saving you real time.

This is **Delegation** again, the first D, at a bigger scale. Knowing what Claude *can* do is half of deciding what to hand over.

## Documents, spreadsheets and slides

Ask for the finished thing, not text you then have to copy and paste:

- "Turn these notes into a one-page Word document with headings."
- "Make me a spreadsheet to track my monthly spending, with categories and totals."
- "Make five slides from this report for a ten-minute talk."

You get a file you can download, open and edit like any other.

## Pages and small tools you can use right there

Claude can also build small interactive things that open right there in the chat:

- a calculator for your own quotes
- a checklist you can tick off
- a quiz to help your child revise
- a planner for a trip, with a day-by-day view

You describe it in plain English. You don't need to know any code. If it isn't right, tell it what to change, just like a document. When you're happy, you can share it with a link.

Some of the [projects on this site](/field-notes/projects/) started exactly like this.

If you find yourself making the same kind of thing again and again, the next guide shows how to teach Claude to do it your way every time.

## A word of caution

The more Claude makes for you, the more it matters to check what it made. Open the spreadsheet and test a few totals. Read the document before you send it. It saves you the making, not the checking.

## Try this today

Think of one thing you keep in your head or on scraps of paper: a list, a budget, a routine, a plan. Ask Claude to turn it into a document or a small tool. Then use it for a week.
