Have you noticed yourself typing the same instructions again and again? "Use British spelling. Keep it under a page. Put the actions at the top." That's a sign you need a skill.

## What a skill is

A **skill** is a saved set of instructions for a job you do regularly. You teach it once. After that, Claude does the job the same way every time it comes up.

In four-Ds terms, a skill is **Description** you only have to write once. All the good habits from guide 3 (the full picture, what good looks like, the method you want) get saved, so you don't have to remember them every time.

## Good jobs for a skill

Anything you do often, the same way each time:

- turning meeting notes into a list of actions, with who and when
- writing a weekly update in the same format
- replying to a common type of enquiry in your house style
- checking a document against your own checklist

If you do it once a month or less, or it's different every time, a skill probably isn't worth it. A good brief will do.

## How to make one

You don't need to write it from scratch, and you don't need any technical knowledge.

1. **Write the job down as about five steps.** In plain English, the way you'd explain it to someone new. "First, read the notes. Then list every decision. Then..."
2. **Ask Claude to turn it into a skill.** "Turn these steps into a skill I can reuse." It will tidy them into clear instructions.
3. **Try it on a real example.** See what it gets right and wrong.
4. **Fix the instructions, not just the answer.** If it gets something wrong, change the skill so it gets it right next time.
5. **Try it on a second example.** If it works twice, it's ready.

Look for **Skills** in Claude's settings to see and manage the ones you have. What's available can depend on your plan, and on your organisation's settings if you use Claude at work.

## Build the checking in

The best skills include their own check. Add a last step such as:

- "Finally, list anything you assumed."
- "Finally, list any facts I should double-check."

That way, Discernment (guide 5) is built into the job.

## A handy way to think about it

Connecting Claude to your other tools is coming up in the next guide. The two work well together:

> **A skill is the recipe. Connections to your tools are the ingredients.**

A skill tells Claude *how* to do the job. Connections give it the information to do it with.

## Try this today

Pick one job you did last week that you'll do again next week. Write it down as five steps. Ask Claude to turn it into a skill, then try it on a real example.
