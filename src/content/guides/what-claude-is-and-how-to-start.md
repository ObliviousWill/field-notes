If you've never used an AI assistant, this is where to start. No technical knowledge needed.

## What it is

Claude is an AI assistant made by a company called Anthropic. You type (or say) something to it in plain English, and it replies in plain English. It can:

- write and rewrite things: emails, letters, posts, reports
- explain things: a contract, a payslip, a topic you need to understand by Friday
- think things through with you: a decision, a plan, a difficult conversation
- read things you give it: a document, a spreadsheet, a photo
- make things: a document, a simple web page, a small tool

It isn't a search engine. A search engine finds pages for you to read. Claude reads, thinks and writes the answer itself. That's why it's so useful, and also why you should check anything important (more on that below).

## How to get it

1. Go to **claude.ai** in your web browser, or download the **Claude app** on your phone (App Store or Google Play) or computer.
2. Sign up with your email address.
3. That's it. You'll see a box to type in.

There's a free version, which is plenty for getting started. Paid plans give you more use each day and some extra features. Start free, and upgrade only if you keep running out.

## Your first ten minutes

Don't start with a test question to see if it's clever. Start with something real from your day. Some ideas:

- "Here's an email I need to reply to. I want to say no politely. Write me a reply." Then paste the email in.
- "I have chicken, rice, a pepper and an onion. What can I cook in 30 minutes?"
- "Explain what an ISA is, as if I'd never heard of one."
- "I've got a meeting with my manager about a pay rise. Help me plan what to say."

Then **reply to its answer**. This is the bit most people skip. If it's too long, say "shorter". If it's too formal, say "more casual". If it got something wrong, tell it. It's a conversation, not a one-off question.

## Three things to know early

**It can be wrong, and sound sure.** Claude is right most of the time, but it can make mistakes and still sound confident. For anything that matters, such as health, money, law or facts you'll repeat, check it. Ask it "how sure are you?" or "where could this be wrong?". It will usually tell you honestly.

**Think before you paste.** Don't share things you wouldn't want stored somewhere else: passwords, bank details, other people's private information. If you're using it for work, check what your employer allows.

**It doesn't know you yet.** Out of the box it gives the answer it would give anyone. The more you tell it about your situation, the better the answer. That's what the next guide is about.

## Try this today

Pick one thing you'd normally spend 20 minutes on: an awkward email, a summary of something long, a plan for the week. Give it to Claude, then go back and forth until it's right. Notice how much of the 20 minutes you got back.
