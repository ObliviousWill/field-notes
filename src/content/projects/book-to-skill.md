When you ask an AI for marketing advice, you get the average of everything it has ever read. That's often fine, and it's rarely sharp.

I wanted Claude to think with two specific books: Alex Hormozi's *$100M Leads* (how to get people interested) and *$100M Offers* (how to make an offer people find hard to say no to). So I turned each book into a **skill**. A skill is a set of reference notes and instructions that Claude reads when the topic comes up.

## What's inside

Each book became a small folder:

- **A short summary** of the main ideas, enough for quick questions
- **One page per chapter**, always in the same shape: the big idea, the methods, common mistakes, a worked example and the key takeaways
- **An index** saying which chapter covers which topic
- **A glossary** of the book's terms
- **A cheat sheet** of rules of thumb for making decisions quickly

I used a free tool that does most of the conversion. I then checked the result and filled in the gaps, because charts and diagrams don't survive being turned into text.

## How Claude uses it

The instructions tell Claude:

- to use the summary for a quick answer
- for "how do I actually do this?" questions, to find the right chapter and read it first
- to apply the ideas to my situation, not just repeat them back
- to treat the author's numbers as his own results, not promises
- to check claims like money-back guarantees and "only three left" against UK advertising rules

The result is the difference between asking a clever stranger and asking someone who has read the book properly and kept their notes.

These are summaries, not the books themselves, and they stay private. If you like the ideas, buy the books.
